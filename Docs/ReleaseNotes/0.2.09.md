### Windows Phone 8 games

**GTA: San Andreas** runs through its own managed page and native engine: menu, a new game, the
intro, and Grove Street with CJ moving on the stick, the HUD and people on the streets. It runs at
30 fps, which is the rate the game locks itself to; a faster frame rate would make it run fast.
Getting there took several general fixes to the Windows Phone 8 runtime, including sharing the CPU
fairly between a game's threads and a clock that stays honest when a thread waits by spinning.

**Asphalt 7: Heat** races. Its reflections and sky were missing, and the world behind the HUD went
black once the race camera took over. Races also use far less memory than before.

**N.O.V.A. 3** plays its campaign, and **Microsoft Bingo** runs its card game instead of only its
particle effects.

**Fruit Ninja (2013) and Jetpack Joyride** had no sound at all; they do now, music and effects.
Their "Exit" button now closes the game, as it did on the phone.

**Games now run their own page.** Many Windows Phone 8 games are a page written in C# with the game
engine drawing inside it. WPR used to stand in for that page; it now runs the game's own, as the phone
did. That is what got **Where's My Perry?** to its menu and episode select, with its sprites drawn
properly instead of in black boxes and its sound engine running, and **Star Wars: Tiny Death Star**
into its tutorial. The three games that used to need a hand-written description of their page
(Amazing Alex, Modern Combat 4 and Assassin's Creed Pirates) no longer do.

### Windows Phone 7 and Silverlight games

- **PAC-MAN Kart Rally** starts and takes taps. It used to fail on a save file it wrote where the
  phone would never have let it. Its local multiplayer can host a game and find one.
- **Galactic Reign** leaves its first-run tutorial video and reaches the menu instead of a black screen.
- **Deer Hunter 3D** gets past "Looking for Updates" to its menu.
- **Star Wars: The Battle for Hoth** shows its HUD and tutorial pop-ups. Games are no longer run faster
  than the phone's screen could show, which was freezing its timers.
- **Final Fantasy**: lifting the virtual pad while holding Dash no longer leaves the pad held down.
- **Touch**: a tap quicker than one frame now registers, games that read touch through the mouse take
  it on Android (Need for Speed Undercover, SPY Mouse), and with two fingers down, lifting the first no
  longer loses its tap in Silverlight games.
- **Live Store** lists its catalogue and opens an app's page. Signing in and downloading apps don't
  work yet.

### Achievements and WPR Hub

- **Achievements for games WPR has no list for.** The list is built from the game's own package the
  first time it starts, and the hub is offered what it doesn't already have. An unlock for an
  achievement no list mentions is still recorded and uploaded.
- **Game tile icons on Android.** A package that names its icon with Windows-style backslashes now
  shows its tile art, and the icon is sent to WPR Hub for games that were sent without one.
- **The hub stops asking for games it already has.** WPR now tells it which version of a game you
  have, so it only asks you to upload one it is missing.

### Known issues

- **GTA: San Andreas** runs at 30 fps by design: that is the game's own rate.
- **Unity games for Windows Phone 8** (Unicorn Ride and others) still show a black screen. Work on
  running Unity's engine is under way.
- **Halo** still draws some objects as black shapes.
- **Application bars** (the row of round buttons at the bottom of some Silverlight pages) still aren't
  drawn.
- **Live Store** can't sign in or download apps.
