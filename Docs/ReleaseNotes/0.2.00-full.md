# WPR 0.2.00: full release notes

The long version: what each change actually is, how it behaves, and what's still missing. The
short summary that goes on the release page is [`0.2.00.md`](0.2.00.md) beside this file.

---

**Your Windows Phone just got its Xbox LIVE back.** Well, sort of. Meet **WPR Hub**: a real online
home for your games, with a gamertag, a gamerscore, friends, messages and leaderboards. It's the
biggest thing WPR has ever shipped, so the version number jumped to **0.2** to celebrate.

Until now, everything WPR knew about you stayed on one device. Achievements were unlocked and then
sat in a database file nobody else would ever see, high scores went nowhere, and when a game broke
on your phone, the only way we found out was if you told us. All three of those have changed.

**Sign in once and you have a gamertag**, and every game you play sees it. **Every achievement
you've ever unlocked in WPR counts**, including the ones from months ago. **High scores go
online**, to real leaderboards. **Friends can see what you're playing**, and you can message them.
And **when a game breaks, it can tell us by itself**.

> **Upgrading is normal.** Install the new APK over the top on Android, or run the installer on
> Windows. Your games and saves stay put.
>
> **Repatch once for the Montezuma music fix.** On Android it happens automatically the first time
> you launch each game. On Windows, press **Repatch** on the game's page, or start WPR with
> `--repatch-installed` to do your whole library. Nothing else in this release needs a repatch.
>
> **Nothing about your account leaves the device until you sign in**, and you never have to.
> Every game still plays exactly as before while signed out. See *What goes online, and when*
> below for the full list.

### Meet WPR Hub

The hub lives in two places:

- On **Android**, it's the **top tile on the Start screen**. Signed out, it's a plain "wpr hub /
  sign in or sign up" tile. Signed in, it turns into your **gamer card**: your gamertag, your
  gamerscore and how many friends are online, with the hub logo in the corner. Tap it for the
  hub page.
- On **Windows**, it's the new **HUB** tab in the sidebar, which now reads PLAY, TROPHIES, HUB,
  MESSAGES, CONTROLS, SETTINGS, ABOUT.

The hub page shows your profile (gamertag, gamerpic, and how many games, how much playtime and
how many achievements you've racked up), any friend requests waiting for you, your friends and
what they're up to, a box to add a friend by gamertag, the requests you've sent, and your games
with their tile art. On Android, tapping an installed game there launches it.

The gamer card costs nothing to draw. Gamerscore is added up from the achievements on your
device, and the friends count comes from the last time WPR checked in with the hub, so the Start
screen never waits on the network.

### Signing in (and signing up, which is the same thing)

There's one screen for both. Choose **Continue with GitHub** or **Continue with Google**, and WPR
shows you a short **link key** and a button that opens the hub's link page in your browser. Sign
in there, check that the key matches, and approve. WPR is waiting the whole time (there's a
countdown so you know how long the key lasts) and picks up the moment you approve.

**A new account picks its gamertag before it's approved.** The link page suggests one based on
your nickname with the provider, and you can change it to whatever you like, within reason. One
name pattern is reserved: `player` followed by digits, because that's what guests are called (see
below), and letting an account take one would mean two people with the same name.

Once you're in, WPR saves your sign-in, turns on server logging for your account to match the
switch in WPR, fetches your gamerpic, and sends anything that was waiting for you: scores you
posted as a guest, achievements, playtime.

**Signing out** is on the hub page (Android) or the Online account row in settings (Windows).

### Your gamertag is what games see

WP7 games ask "who's playing?" all the time: for a high-score table, a save-file name, a "Welcome
back, ___!" banner. The answer is now:

- **Signed in**: your hub gamertag, and your hub gamerpic as your picture.
- **Signed out**: a **guest gamertag**, `player` plus six digits, made up the first time WPR runs
  and kept for good. Games show the bundled default picture.

The guest name never leaves your device. Scores you post as a guest wait on the device and go up
under your real account when you sign in.

**The old gamertag and picture settings are gone from both settings pages**, because there's
nothing left for them to do. If you'd typed a gamertag in there before, games will use your hub
name or your guest name instead.

### Achievements: the back catalogue goes up

**Every unlock is still recorded on your device first**, exactly as before. What's new is that
each one is now marked "waiting to upload" until the hub has confirmed it. Signed in and online,
that's a matter of seconds. Offline, signed out, or the upload failed? Then it waits, and goes up
the next time WPR can send it. Nothing is lost, and nothing is sent twice.

**None of your earlier unlocks had ever been sent anywhere**, so this release marks all of them as
waiting, once. The first time you sign in, your whole history goes up in one batch, and your
gamerscore starts from where you actually are. On our test phone that was ten achievements in one
go, and the hub agreed it had never seen any of them. Very satisfying.

Each achievement is sent with **how long you'd played that game before unlocking it**, which is
where playtime comes in.

### Playtime

WPR now keeps track of how long you play each game. A session starts when the game starts and ends
when it closes. It **pauses while the game is in the background** and picks up again when you come
back, so leaving a game sitting behind your browser doesn't count. Sessions shorter than five
seconds are thrown away as launches that didn't really happen.

Playtime is saved to your device every minute, so a crash costs you at most a minute of it. It
shows on your profile, on your friends' profiles, and alongside each achievement.

### Leaderboards

Games that had Xbox LIVE leaderboards on the phone now post to real ones on the hub. You don't do
anything: when the game writes a score, WPR sends it, and when the game asks for the board, WPR
fetches it. All three views a game can ask for work (top scores, you and the people around you,
and your friends), with paging for the games that scroll.

A few details:

- **Signed out, the game sees an empty board**, the same as before, and your scores wait on the
  device. They're sent the first time you sign in.
- **A board that fails to load comes back empty**, never as an error in the game. WP7 games were
  not good at coping with an online error, and an empty board is something every one of them
  already knew how to show.
- **Boards are created when a game first asks for them**, so the very first player of a game gets
  a board ready and waiting. Whether a board ranks highest-first or lowest-first is guessed from
  its name until someone defines it properly: names that mean "best score" rank high scores
  first, and names that mean "best time" rank low times first.

### Friends and presence

Add a friend by gamertag. They get a request, and once they accept, you each appear in the other's
friends list along with what you're doing: **online**, **playing *Cut the Rope***, or offline and
when you were last seen.

While you're signed in, WPR checks in with the hub about once a minute, saying whether you're in
a game and which one. On Android, the launcher and the game run as two separate processes, and
the launcher deliberately goes quiet while a game is running, or your friends would see you
flicker between "online" and "playing" every thirty seconds.

**Tap a friend for their profile**: their gamerpic and status, their totals (games, playtime,
achievements, gamerscore), and their most recent unlocks and play sessions.

On Android, friends and messages share one place now: the **social** tile on the Start screen
opens two pivots, **friends** and **messages**. If you have unread messages it opens straight
onto messages.

### Messages

You can message any of your friends. On Android it's the **messages** pivot of the social screen;
on Windows it's the new **MESSAGES** tab, with your conversations on the left and the open one on
the right.

- **Messages are between friends only.** Type a gamertag you're not friends with, and WPR offers
  to send a friend request instead.
- **If you stop being friends, the conversation stays** but becomes read-only.
- **A conversation that's open checks for new messages every few seconds**, and marks them read
  as you see them.
- **Long-press a message** to copy it, delete it for yourself, or report it. Reporting also lets
  you block the sender.

The tile counts (friends online, unread messages) catch up within seconds of you coming back to
WPR, because the launcher checks in again as soon as it returns to the front.

### Appear offline (Android)

Want to play without your friends seeing? Turn on **appear offline** at the top of the friends
pivot. Your friends see you as offline, with a "last seen" time from before you switched it on.
You can still send and read messages, and everything else works as normal.

Your play sessions and unlocks while you're hidden don't show up on your profile's recent
activity either. One thing to know: **your totals still count everything**, so a sharp-eyed friend
might notice your gamerscore going up while you look offline. We decided that was fine.

### Gamerpics

Pick a gamerpic from the hub's set on the hub page. It's saved on your device and **becomes your
picture in every game** that shows one. Sign out and games go back to the bundled default.

The old "choose a picture from a file" option and the strip of default avatars on Windows are
gone. There's no local picture to keep any more: your picture is your hub picture.

### Crash reports

When a game goes wrong, WPR now writes a crash report and sends it to the hub. It catches three
kinds of failure:

- **An error that escapes a game while it's running**, on either platform.
- **A crash on a background thread** that takes the whole app down.
- On Android, **a game that dies without a managed error at all** (a native crash, or the system
  killing it). That one is noticed by the launcher when the game process disappears.

**Reports are never sent from inside the crash.** A dying game is the worst place to try to use
the network, so WPR writes the report to a file and sends it later: the next time WPR starts, and
after every game. That means a report always gets through, even after the worst kind of crash.

Reports include the game's log from that run and some details about your device and its graphics
driver, which is usually what tells us why. They're gated by the **server logging** switch in
settings, which is **on** by default. Turn it off and nothing is sent.

**This only works because WPR now keeps a log in release builds.** Until now, the useful logging
only existed in debug builds, and the release app on Windows threw its output away. WPR now keeps
a small rolling log on disk (capped at a few megabytes), and that's what reports are built from.

### Play with logging

Got a game that's broken in a way that doesn't crash? It hangs, or draws the wrong thing, or
ignores your taps? **Play with logging** is for that.

- On **Android**, long-press the game in the games list and choose **play with logging**.
- On **Windows**, press **Play with logging** on the game's page.

The game starts with extra logging switched on, and a **stop & send report** button floats over it.
Play until the thing goes wrong, then tap the button **twice** (twice so you can't send one by
accident). WPR closes the game, sends everything that was logged during the run (on Android, the
system log too), and shows you a **report code**. Quote that code when you tell us about it, and
we can go straight to your report.

If the game exits or crashes by itself during the run, the report is sent anyway. On Windows, a
game that won't close within eight seconds of you pressing stop is reported while it's still
running.

Asking for a logging run counts as consent, so these reports are sent even with server logging
turned off.

This replaces the old "send a report" box in settings, which sent whatever had happened in the
last twenty minutes. That window rarely held the moment that mattered.

### Game files

When you install a game, WPR now takes a fingerprint of the package you installed it from and
tells the hub. The fingerprint is how the hub knows exactly which build of a game you have, which
matters because a lot of WP7 games came in several versions, and the installed copy can't tell us
afterwards: it's been patched.

**If the hub doesn't have that build yet, it can ask for a copy**, and WPR will upload it, after
checking that the file still matches the fingerprint. That's how we get hold of the builds people
are actually playing. This goes through the same **server logging** switch as crash reports: turn
it off and neither the fingerprint nor the file is sent.

Games installed **before** this release have no fingerprint, so the hub can't ask for them.
Reinstalling a game gives it one.

### "How did it run?"

Every so often, when you finish a game, WPR asks how it ran and offers six answers, best first:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Perfect** | No problems at all |
| **Playable** | Minor glitches, but it can be played to the end |
| **In-game** | Gets into the game, but with serious problems |
| **Menus** | Reaches the menus, but not into the game |
| **Boots** | Starts, then crashes or hangs |
| **Nothing** | Doesn't launch |

Your answer goes to the hub's compatibility list, which is how we know which games to look at
next. It's polite about it:

- It only asks when you're **signed in**, after a run that **lasted at least a minute** and
  **ended normally**.
- It asks **at most once an hour** by default. Choose how often in settings (**Ask how a game
  ran**): after every game, every 30 minutes, once an hour, every 3 hours, once a day, once a
  week, or never.
- **Ask me later** (or just closing the question) asks about the same game again next time.
  **Don't ask about this game** means never again on this version of WPR.
- Once you've rated a game, it won't ask about that game again until the next WPR version, when
  the answer might have changed.

### Updates and what's new (Android)

**WPR now tells you when there's a new release.** It checks GitHub at most twice a day, and posts
one notification per release. Tap it to go to the About page, where the update row downloads the
new APK. Don't want the automatic check? There's an **UPDATES** switch in settings. The About page
can still check on demand.

**The About page has a what's new pivot**, which shows the short version of these notes for the
version you have installed, with earlier versions underneath. They're built into the app, so they
work offline.

### The Treasures of Montezuma's music switched itself off

Set the music volume, start a level, and the music went to zero. Every level, every time.

The game pauses its own music for a moment at the start of each level, while it plays a jingle,
then carries on. Its options screen is listening for the music being paused, which on the phone
meant the *player* had paused it from the phone's own music controls, and it moved the volume
slider to zero to match. It couldn't tell the game's own pause from yours, and moving the slider
saved the volume. So the jingle finished, the music resumed, and it resumed at nothing.

Nothing outside a game can pause WPR's music player, so that listener only ever saw the game's own
pauses. WPR now switches it off when the game is patched. **This needs a repatch** (see the box at
the top).

### Smaller things

- **A new intro video** plays when WPR starts on Android.
- **WPR's own buttons no longer sit under Android's navigation bar.** On phones with on-screen
  buttons, the bottom of the screen used to be partly hidden behind them.
- **The per-game info screen has gone**, on both platforms. Everything it showed about graphics
  and the last launch is now in the reports above, which is where it was actually useful.
- **The hub address isn't in settings.** Everyone uses the official hub.
- **The trophies page names more games properly on Windows.** A game that wasn't in WPR's built-in
  list used to show as a long string of letters and numbers. Windows now asks the hub for the name
  and remembers the answer.
- **The Android settings page has no account section.** Sign in, sign out and gamerpics are all
  on the hub tile now.

### What goes online, and when

All in one place:

| | sent when |
| --- | --- |
| **Achievements, scores, playtime** | only while signed in |
| **"I'm online / playing X"** | only while signed in, about once a minute (hidden if you appear offline) |
| **Messages and friend requests** | only when you send them |
| **Ratings** | only when you answer the question |
| **Crash reports** | automatically, unless you turn **server logging** off |
| **Game fingerprints, and game files the hub asks for** | on install, unless you turn **server logging** off |
| **Play with logging reports** | only when you start a logging run, even with server logging off |
| **Update checks** (Android) | twice a day at most, unless you turn **UPDATES** off. This asks GitHub, not the hub |

### What's still missing

- **Windows has no update check or what's new page yet.** The code for both is shared, so this is
  mostly a matter of building the screens.
- **Appear offline is Android only.** Windows doesn't have the switch yet.
- **The Android trophies page doesn't ask the hub for game names yet**, only Windows does.
- **Messages only go between friends.** That's on purpose.
